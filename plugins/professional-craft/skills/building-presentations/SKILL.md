# Building presentations

Follow `references/style-guide.md` for voice and terminology.

A deck is a means to an outcome: after it, the audience believes or does something
specific. Design backwards from that.

## Process

1. **Define the outcome.** Audience, what they believe now, what they should believe or
   do at the end, and how long you have. If the outcome is a decision, see
   `briefing-decisions` for the logic and use the deck to carry it.
2. **Write the storyline first** as slide headlines only. Each headline is a full
   takeaway sentence ("Churn fell 12% after the onboarding change"), never a topic
   label ("Churn"). Read them in order: they should make the whole argument.
3. **Anticipate the room.** List the three hardest questions this audience will ask
   and make sure the storyline answers them or an appendix slide does.
4. **One idea per slide.** Under each headline, one visual or at most three short
   bullets that prove it. Every number has its source or basis on the slide.
5. **Pick visuals by content**: a chart for change over time or comparison, a table for
   exact values, a diagram for a flow, a screenshot for proof something happened. If a
   sentence says it, skip the visual.
6. **Write speaker notes** with what to say, not what's on the slide.
7. **Close with the ask** or the one thing to remember, and what happens next.
8. **Rehearsal check.** Read the headlines alone in under a minute: does the argument
   land? Time the full run against the slot at about one to two minutes per slide.
   Move what doesn't fit to the appendix.

## Quality bar

- Headlines alone tell the story.
- No slide has more than one point.
- The outcome slide states a concrete ask with an owner or next step.
