# Researching topics

Research is for a decision or an action. Aim it there, and make it checkable.

## Process

1. **Pin the question to its use.** What will the person decide or do with the answer?
   That sets scope, depth and the date range that matters. Write the question in one
   sentence before searching.
2. **Plan the sources.** The person's own documents and apps first when the question is
   about their organisation; then primary sources (official sites, filings, papers,
   documentation, data); then reputable secondary analysis. Search summaries and other
   AIs' answers are leads only.
3. **Search iteratively.** Let each finding shape the next search. Record each source
   with what it says, its date, and how authoritative it is.
4. **Check what's current.** For anything that changes (prices, people, rules, versions,
   rankings), confirm with the most recent primary source and note the date.
5. **Look for disagreement on purpose.** Search for the counter-case to your emerging
   answer. Where sources conflict, show both, say which is stronger and why.
6. **Synthesise, don't list.** Lead with the answer to the question, then the evidence
   for it, then what's uncertain. Group by finding, not by source.
7. **Grade confidence.** For each key finding: high (several independent primary
   sources agree), medium (one primary or several secondary), low (thin, old or
   conflicting). Say what would raise it.
8. **Cite everything load-bearing** and end with a Sources list. Run `tracing-claims`
   checks if installed.

## Output

```
Answer: <one or two sentences, with overall confidence>
Key findings: <finding — evidence — confidence>, three to seven of them
Where sources disagree: <conflict and your reading>
Gaps: <what couldn't be found or verified, and how to close it>
Sources: <title — link — date>
```

## Quality bar

- The first two sentences answer the question asked, not a nearby one.
- No finding rests only on a secondary summary when a primary source exists.
- Dates are given for anything time-sensitive.
- Nothing is invented to fill a gap; gaps are named.
