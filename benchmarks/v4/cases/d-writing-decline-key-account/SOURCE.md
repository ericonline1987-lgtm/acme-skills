## Grader basis

- no-is-up-front: BLUF and plain-language "main point first" (Wikipedia BLUF; digital.gov principles; HBR on stating the purpose first).
- no-false-hope: Readability Guidelines on vague commitment words ("commit", "pledge": state whether you are doing something or not); plain-language practice of being specific.
- alternatives-tied-to-rollout: BLUF structure (bottom line plus required action/next step); writing for the reader's actual goal (digital.gov "write for your audience").
- no-stock-filler: Readability Guidelines on vague phrases; TechCrunch summary of Wikipedia's observations about formulaic, polished-but-empty machine prose.
- no-invented-commitments: plain-language specificity; standard account-management practice of not promising what has not been agreed (practitioner judgment).

## Why a capable general model might miss it

Models trained toward politeness tend to cushion and hedge refusals to important customers, which reads as a maybe and fills the email with stock courtesy phrases.

The customer, numbers and notes in prompt.md are synthetic.
