A senior ML/evals practitioner starts from error analysis: read a few hundred real drafts, write down how they fail, and turn those failures into specific binary checks (policy accuracy, invented refunds/actions, wrong escalation, data leakage, tone in abuse cases), some code-checkable, some LLM-judged with a judge calibrated against human labels. The eval set is stratified with deliberate oversampling of the rare high-risk tags and known hard cases, results are reported per category, the gate has zero-tolerance criteria for severe failures and compares against the current version with enough samples to see real differences, and the agent-in-the-loop gives a production signal (edit distance, discard rate, CSAT). A competent generalist suggests "more samples", "a rubric", maybe human spot-checks, but keeps similarity-to-agent-reply as the target and an averaged score as the ship gate.
