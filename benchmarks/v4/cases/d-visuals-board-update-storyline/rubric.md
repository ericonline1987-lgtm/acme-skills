A senior strategy or finance lead builds the deck as an argument: slide 1 is the executive summary — "growing 31% but 3% behind plan; the miss is SMB churn; we propose shifting $1.5M to enterprise and raising the SMB minimum" — and the following slides each carry a one-sentence action title that proves one step (where we missed, why: SMB churn by segment and NRR trend, what's working: enterprise pipeline and AI routing adoption, the plan and its cost, the two asks with numbers), leaving discussion time. Reading only the titles tells the whole story. A competent generalist produces a conventional sequence (agenda, highlights, financials, product, people, asks) with topic-label titles, leads with wins, and puts the asks on the last slide disconnected from the churn evidence.
