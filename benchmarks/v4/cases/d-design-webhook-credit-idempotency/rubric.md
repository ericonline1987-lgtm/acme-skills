A senior payments engineer redesigns around an append-only credit ledger: verify the signature, insert the event (or payment-intent / refund ID) into a table with a unique constraint in the same transaction as the ledger entry, return 2xx fast and do the work from a queue, compute balances atomically from the ledger, treat each refund by its refund ID and amount (charge.refunded includes partial refunds and can repeat), tolerate out-of-order and duplicate-but-distinct events by looking at object state, send the receipt after commit with its own dedupe, and run a reconciliation against provider records to repair the 37 over-credited and the negative accounts. A competent generalist adds "check event ID before processing" and maybe a queue, but leaves the non-atomic `+=`, the check-then-act race, and the partial-refund over-deduction in place, and forgets to repair existing data.
