A senior engineer treats this as a correctness and load problem, not just latency: key on sku×region×tier(×currency); cache for display only and re-price authoritatively at checkout; replace the plain 10-minute TTL with change-driven invalidation plus a TTL backstop, and pre-load the flash-sale prices with effective times since they're known a week ahead; prevent a stampede at 12:00:00 with request coalescing, stale-while-revalidate and jitter; and plan for the cache being cold or down because pricing-service cannot absorb 8k rps (local cache, load shedding, concurrency caps). They may also note 120k×24 keys is fine for Redis but that hit rate is dominated by the hot 2k SKUs. A competent generalist endorses Redis with TTL tweaks and maybe invalidation, but misses the key dimensions, the checkout correctness boundary, or the cold-cache/stampede failure modes.
