A senior data-visualisation practitioner says no to the 90–100% clustered bars (length encodes value, so a 93% bar would look half the size of a 97% one) and redraws it as a dumbbell/dot plot or slope chart from Q2 to Q3, sorted by change, with Columbus and Joliet in an accent colour, everything else grey, and an action title like "Columbus and Joliet slipped ~2 pts in Q3". They also notice Savannah's tiny volume makes its +4.3 pts misleading and footnote or separate it, and may translate the slips into extra late orders (~5,300 at Columbus). A competent generalist often accepts the truncated axis with a caveat or "axis break", or proposes a fix but misses the Savannah denominator problem.
